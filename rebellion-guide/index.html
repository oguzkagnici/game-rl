Learning guide · Star Wars: Rebellion base game · 2 players

One side is hunting. The other is running out the clock.

Rebellion is two different games sharing one board. Most of what feels confusing in the rulebook makes sense once you know what each side is actually trying to do. This guide teaches that first, then the rules that serve it, then lets you practise decisions against a scripted opponent before your first real game.

The Galactic Empire

Wins by: conquering the hidden Rebel base

  • Starts huge: fleets, troops, a Death Star.
  • Does not know where the base is. Learns it from probe cards, missions and clues.
  • Has a deadline. Every round that passes is one it doesn't get back.

The Rebel Alliance

Wins by: making the time and reputation markers meet

  • Starts small and can't win a straight war.
  • Wins by staying hidden long enough and scoring objectives to speed up the clock.
  • Fights only when a fight pays: an objective, a freed system, a saved base.

Concept 1 · The clock

Every reputation point is a round the Empire loses

The time track runs along the board. The time marker starts on space 1 and moves forward one space at the end of every round. The reputation marker starts on space 14. When the Rebels score objectives, reputation moves toward the time marker. The moment both markers share a space, the Rebels win.

So the number that matters all game is the gap between them. Try it below.

rounds left for the Empire if the Rebels never score. Each round the time marker steps forward once. Each objective the Rebels score pulls reputation closer, so a Rebel who scores one point every round closes the gap twice as fast.

What this means for play. The Rebels should treat objectives as their real army. The Empire should check the gap every single round and ask: can my current plan find the base in that many rounds?

Concept 2 · Shape of a round

Three phases, and what to think about in each

Every round has the same three phases in the same order. For each step you get the rule and, more usefully, what a good player is thinking at that moment.

Concept 3 · Leaders are actions

You have only as many actions as you have leaders

Every leader you own does exactly one thing per round. Either it goes on a mission during Assignment, or it stays in your pool and later does one of two things: activate a system (move units, start a battle) or oppose one enemy mission. Once used, it stays on the board until the round ends.

The pinning rule

You cannot move units out of a system that already holds one of your own leaders. That includes a leader you placed there to oppose a mission or to attempt one.

Use it as a weapon: attempt a mission in the system where your opponent's fleet sits. If they oppose, their leader lands on their own fleet and freezes it for the rest of the round. If they don't, your mission succeeds for free.

Tempo: the last word. Turns alternate during the Command Phase, Rebels first. When one player runs out of leaders or passes, the other takes all remaining actions in a row with full information. Keeping one more leader in reserve than your opponent often means you make the last, best-informed move of the round.

Playbook

Rebel Alliance

Play the clock, not the war

Objectives are your army

Reputation is the only resource that wins. Before every Assignment Phase look at your objective hand and ask which one you can finish soonest. Pick missions and fights that move a card forward. Everything else is secondary.

Hide the base away from the action

Choose a base far from the Imperial core and also away from where your own fleet will operate. The Empire watches your fleet. Distance costs the Empire activations and transports, and those are rounds.

The base space leaks information

Moving units to or from the Rebel Base space tells the Empire the base is in that system or next to it. Do your fighting with a separate mobile fleet and keep base movement for when it's worth the clue.

Keep a reserve to oppose

An "attempt" mission succeeds automatically unless you send a leader. Captures and base-hunting missions are the ones to stop. The opposer wins ties, so even one die is a real chance.

Bait the pin

Attempt missions where Imperial fleets are parked. Opposing puts their leader there and freezes that fleet for the round. Either you get the mission or you buy a round of safety.

Loyalty is your factory

Rebels only build in loyal systems (and the base). Diplomacy early gives you units every build round. Turning an Imperial-loyal system neutral also takes production away from them.

Sabotage before the build

A sabotaged system can't build or receive new units. Time it just before a build round, ideally on a system that makes Imperial troops, which they need to search for you.

Fight only when it pays

You can't out-trade the Empire. Take a battle when it scores an objective, frees a subjugated system, or saves the base. Bring a leader so you can retreat once you've got what you came for.

Relocate before contact

Rapid Mobilization moves the base, but your units in the base space are left at the old, now-revealed location and the new base starts empty. Do it before Imperial troops arrive; you take the first turn of each Command Phase.

Guard your leaders

A Rebel leader standing in a system with an Imperial unit can be targeted by capture missions. Know where your mission leaders will end up and whether you can afford to defend them.

Playbook

Galactic Empire

Search two ways at once, and never forget the deadline

Count the gap every round

Reputation minus time is how many rounds you have, and it shrinks faster when the Rebels score. Every plan should fit inside that number.

Presence rules out systems

When your ground units enter a system, the Rebels must say if the base is there. Every system you occupy with troops is a system ruled out, and subjugated systems also build for you.

Ground units find bases

Ships alone never reveal the base. Only Imperial ground units or Imperial loyalty do. Plan fleets around transport capacity; TIE Fighters need transport too.

Information narrows the map

You draw two probe cards every round, and some missions draw more. Each card names a system where the base is not. Keep a written list and combine it with what you see.

Read Rebel movement

Units moving to or from the Rebel Base space mean the base is in or next to that system. Where they deploy, defend and refuse to fight are clues too.

Oppose selectively

Opposing a mission parks your leader in that system and pins your units there. Oppose missions that score objectives or cost you production; let cheap decoys go.

Capture, then exploit

Captured Rebel leaders can't help their side and can be targeted by your missions for information. Keep a unit with the captive: if no Imperial units remain in that system, the leader is rescued.

Use the Death Star early

Only the Death Star Plans objective can destroy it, so early on it's nearly untouchable. Use it to spearhead sweeps. Later, escort it with TIE Fighters, and remember a fleet with it can't retreat.

First-game setup at a glance

Both players

  1. Time marker on 1, reputation marker on 14.
  2. Objective deck: III at the bottom, II on it, I on top. Rebels draw one.
  3. Each side takes its 4 leaders without a recruit icon. Recruit-icon leaders wait beside the board.
  4. Action decks: only cards with a recruit icon. Ignore their abilities in game one.
  5. Each side keeps its 4 starting missions (curved arrow) and draws 2 more. Imperial project cards form their own deck.
  6. Remove Corellia, Mandalore, Saleucami, Sullust and Mustafar from the probe deck. Rebels secretly pick a base not adjacent to Imperial units.

Imperial units

  • Coruscant: Star Destroyer, Assault Carrier, 2 TIE, 3 Stormtroopers, AT-AT, AT-ST
  • Mandalore: Death Star, 4 TIE, 2 Stormtroopers, AT-ST, subjugated
  • Saleucami: Star Destroyer, 2 TIE, 2 Stormtroopers, AT-ST, loyal
  • Sullust: Star Destroyer, 2 TIE, 2 Stormtroopers, AT-ST, subjugated
  • Mustafar: Assault Carrier, 2 TIE, 2 Stormtroopers, AT-ST, loyal
  • Corellia: Assault Carrier, 1 Stormtrooper, loyal

Rebel units

  • Bothawui: Corellian Corvette, Rebel Transport, X-wing, Y-wing, 3 Rebel Troopers, Airspeeder, loyal
  • Rebel Base space: X-wing, Y-wing, 3 Rebel Troopers, Airspeeder
