Spar with a bot, with a coach at your shoulder

An original mini-game built to train the decisions that decide Star Wars: Rebellion: hiding or hunting a base, reading the clock, spending leaders as actions, opposing missions, and choosing when to relocate. The map, names and rules are simplified and invented. The coach explains each idea as it comes up in play and tells you how it carries over to the real game. A match takes about 15 minutes.

Reference

Drill rules on one screen

Winning

Dominion wins by landing troops in the hidden base's system and leaving no Insurgent troops alive there.

Insurgents win when the time marker reaches the reputation marker. Time starts at 1 and moves up one space every round. Reputation starts at 14 and moves down when the Insurgents complete objectives (at most one per round, checked at the end of the round).

A round

  1. Assignment. Both sides secretly send leaders on missions or keep them in reserve.
  2. Command. Players alternate, Insurgents first. On your turn, either reveal a mission and pick its target, or use a reserve leader to activate a system, or pass. The phase ends when both pass in a row.
  3. Refresh. Objectives are checked, the Dominion draws a probe card, every loyal system builds a unit, leaders return, time advances.

Activation and pinning

Put a reserve leader in a system. Your units in adjacent systems may move in; each ship carries two troops. If enemy units are there, a battle follows: every unit rolls a die and hits on 4+, ships against ships, troops against troops, and the leaders present add their tactics as extra dice.

Pinning: units in a system that holds one of your leaders cannot move out for the rest of the round. That includes leaders sent there to attempt or oppose a mission.

The hidden base

The base's units sit off the map. They can move out to the base system or next to it, which tells the Dominion roughly where the base is. Dominion troops or Dominion loyalty in the base system reveal it. Revealed base defenders hit on 3+.

Missions

    Missions succeed automatically unless the other side opposes with a reserve leader. Then each rolls intrigue dice (4+ succeeds) and the attempt must roll more successes. Ties go to the opposer.

    Left out on purpose

    The Death Star and Imperial projects, action cards, tactic cards, unit types and their costs, the build queue, retreat, rescue missions and the combat dice system. Learn those from the Field Manual and the rulebook. The drill exists to make the strategic shape of the game feel familiar before your first real session.

    About the bots

    Both bots are scripted. They score each option with the same ideas the coach teaches and show their reasoning in the log. The Insurgent bot never mentions where its base is. Their numbers were tuned with 500 bot-against-bot games: the Insurgents win about 58% at Veteran level, and games last 7 to 9 rounds. "Recruit" makes noisier choices and opposes less often.
